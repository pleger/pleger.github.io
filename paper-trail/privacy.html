Paper Trail privacy

Updated: November 22, 2026

Paper Trail is a research collaboration workspace administered by Paul Leger and administrators designated by the team. For questions about data and access, contact pleger@ucn.cl.

Data used

The application uses Google sign-in and your account email to identify you. Google may provide your name and profile photo to the authentication service. Paper Trail does not request access to Gmail, Google Drive, contacts, or calendars.

Administrators record names, professional email addresses, ORCID identifiers, and permissions. The team records paper titles and descriptions, participants, repositories, statuses, deadlines, tickets, and activity. Researcher identities are associated with events to track work.

Access and sharing

Only active researchers who have accepted an invitation can access the workspace. All active members can view team information. Administrators manage access, permissions, paper settings, and notifications. Paper participants and administrators can work on their tickets and statuses. Invitation links are restricted to one email address, expire after 14 days, and are consumed on acceptance.

Services used

GitHub Pages hosts the public interface. Supabase stores data and manages sessions and shared updates. Google authenticates your account. If email is enabled, Gmail or Resend processes recipient addresses and notification text, which may include paper or ticket titles and links to the application.

The interface loads fonts from Google Fonts and the Supabase library from esm.sh. These providers receive the technical requests needed to deliver their resources. Hosting providers may process IP addresses and technical logs under their own policies.

Sessions and storage

The browser stores the Supabase session and a local marker for read activity. You can sign out from the application. An administrator's view changes do not change the session identity. Local test mode is not used to store papers or researchers.

Retention and deletion

Research information is retained while the team maintains the workspace and its history. Removing a researcher in the application disables access, removes participation, and unassigns their tasks, while preserving their record and historical contributions. Contact the workspace administrator to request correction, export, or deletion. Administrators must also consider data retained in backups and provider retention periods.
